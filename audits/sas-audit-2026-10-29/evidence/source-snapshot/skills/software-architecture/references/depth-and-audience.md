# Intent, depth, audience and execution scope

Intent is design/review/decision/repository/change/handoff. Depth is Quick advice/Guided design/Full specification. Audience is Beginner/Technical. These controls are independent; old Quick Design/Deep Design/Architecture Audit labels are legacy vocabulary, not extra modes. Explain only controls relevant to the task.

Quick advice answers the decision directly without forcing saved context, a diagram or an interview. Guided design includes decisive constraints, proportional design, alternatives/risks and staged action. Full specification adds relevant contracts, traceability, NFR scenarios and exact readiness/verification. Beginner language preserves the same engineering controls. Brevity never means inventing confidence or dropping a consequential unknown.

Retrieve only decision-relevant evidence from selected sources; record contradictory inputs and ask at most two pivotal unanswered questions. Mark available budgets enforced/observed-only/advisory/unsupported. The local toolkit can enforce file/count/worker bounds; a skill cannot guarantee host-wide token bills, agent cancellation or permissions. Preserve completed evidence when a tool is absent or a budget stops work. Never imply an unavailable capability ran.
